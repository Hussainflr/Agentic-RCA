# Maintenance SOP Excerpts

## High Torque With Elevated Tool Wear

When torque increases while tool wear is also high, maintenance should inspect cutting tools, bearings, couplings, alignment, feed settings, and process load. Confirm that the torque sensor is calibrated before assigning root cause. If abnormal mechanical resistance is suspected, isolate the equipment and follow site lockout/tagout procedures before inspection.

## Heat Dissipation Concerns

Elevated process temperature relative to air temperature can indicate cooling flow problems, blocked vents, fouled heat exchangers, high ambient conditions, or load-driven heat generation. Check coolant flow, air paths, fans, thermal interfaces, and recent process changes.

## Power Demand Concerns

Abnormal combinations of torque and rotational speed may indicate excessive power draw, drivetrain load, motor control issues, or sensor calibration error. Compare with motor current, drive alarms, and maintenance history.

## Evidence Handling

Dataset labels and anomaly scores are screening evidence. They should be combined with operator logs, physical inspection, and OEM troubleshooting steps before approving corrective maintenance.
